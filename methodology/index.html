Methodology & standards

The Vespera doctrine

Four disciplines of thought run through every engagement. They are how a calm day is engineered, and how a difficult one is contained.

Intelligence · Advance · Presence · Continuity

Operational doctrine

Four phases, one continuous loop.

Intelligence

Know the picture before anyone moves.

Every program begins with understanding: who the principal is, what attention they attract, where they are going and what the open-source picture reveals. Analysts brief the field team so decisions in the moment are informed, not guessed.

  • Principal and family exposure profile
  • Itinerary and venue threat review
  • Open-source and social monitoring
  • Jurisdiction, legal and medical considerations

Advance

Walk every step before the principal does.

Advance agents travel ahead to drive routes at the hour they will be used, walk venues, meet hotel and venue security, identify safe rooms and plot the nearest trauma centers. Nothing is assumed from a map.

  • Primary, alternate and emergency routes
  • Venue surveys and safe-room selection
  • Partner and local liaison
  • Emergency action plans rehearsed on site

Presence

Close, calm and largely unnoticed.

On the day, officers hold a posture matched to the threat and to the principal's life. They are trained to de-escalate, to move people early and quietly, and to treat discretion as part of the protection rather than an afterthought.

  • Posture set by threat, not habit
  • Protective drivers and vehicles
  • Live tracking by the Operations Desk
  • Medical readiness on every detail

Continuity

Plan for the worst day, then rehearse it.

Programs are reviewed after every movement and tested every quarter. Crisis plans for medical emergencies, evacuation and extortion are written with the family's advisors and practiced, so a bad day becomes a managed one.

  • Written debrief after every operation
  • Quarterly testing and red-team walk-ins
  • Crisis and evacuation plans
  • Insurer and counsel coordination

People

Vetting and training benchmarks.

The standards every Vespera officer meets before their first assignment, and keeps meeting after it.

Selection

Backgrounds in government protective details, specialist policing, military and intelligence work, assessed for judgment, temperament and discretion through structured interviews and scenario exercises.

Vetting

Identity, right-to-work, criminal record and service history verification within local law, independent references and periodic re-vetting throughout employment.

Medical

Advanced pre-hospital trauma and cardiac care certification for at least one officer on every detail, refreshed on a fixed cycle.

Driving

Defensive and evasive driving qualification for protective drivers, with annual requalification and vehicle-specific training for armored platforms.

De-escalation

Conflict de-escalation, fixated-person awareness and legal powers training, because most incidents are best resolved without force.

Supervision

Field supervision by senior leads, written debriefs after every operation and confidential channels for clients to raise concerns.

Duty officers follow live operations, monitor news and weather along routes and coordinate the response when anything changes.

Intelligence cell

Medical line
